<h3>Abstract</h3>

An induced packing of cycles in a graph is a set of vertex-disjoint cycles with no edges between them. The classic Erdős-Pósa theorem shows that for any graph $G$, one can either find $k$ vertex-disjoint cycles or a set of $O(k\*log k)$ vertices which hits every cycles of $G$. Recently, we generalise this classic Erdős-Pósa theorem to induced packings of cycles. We show that there exists a function $f(k)=O(k\*log k)$ such that for every positive integer $k$, every graph $G$ contains either an induced packing of $k$ cycles or a set $X$ of at most $f(k)$ vertices such that the closed neighbourhood of $X$ intersects all cycles in $G$.

In this talk, we introduce a further progress on this topic. We show that the coarse Erdös-Pósa theorem also holds for long cycles. More specifically, we show that there exists a function $f(k,\\ell)=O(k\*\\ell\*\\log k)$ such that for all positive integers $k$ and $\\ell$, every graph $G$ contains either an induced packing of $k$ cycles of length at least $\\ell$ or a set $X$ of at most $f(k,\\ell)$ vertices such that the closed neighbourhood of $X$ intersects all cycles of length at least $\\ell$ in $G$. Our proof is constructive and yields a polynomial-time algorithm finding either the induced packing of long cycles or the set $X$.

This is a joint work with Pascal Gollin, Tony Huynh, and O-joung Kwon.
