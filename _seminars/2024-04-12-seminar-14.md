TITLE : Twin-width of subdivisions

ABSTRACT : Twin-width is a graph parameter which captures a way of recursively identifying vertices with similar neighborhoods. It encompasses many well-known width parameters; for instance, bounded tree-width or rank-width graphs have bounded twin-width. Contrary to tree-width, twin-width is sensitive to taking subdivisions. For $d&lt;\\sqrt{n-1}$, Bonnet, Geniet, Kim, Thomass\\'{e}, and Watrigant showed that the $1$-subdivision of a complete graph $K\_n$ has twin-width at least~$d$, while $K\_n$ itself has twin-width~$0$. On the other hands, Berg\\'{e}, Bonnet, and D\\'{e}pr\\'{e}s showed that for any $n$-vertex graph~$G$, if we subdivide each edge at least $2\\log n-1$ times, then the subdivided graph twin-width at most~$4$.

In this talk, we present the full list of forbidden minors of the graphs where if we subdivide each of their edges at least~$2$ times, then the subdivided graph has twin-width at most~$d$ for each $d\\leq3$. As applications, we compute the twin-width large grid, large wall, and the line graph of large wall, and bound the tree-width of some sparse graphs of twin-width at most 3.

This is a joint work with Debsoumya Chakraborti, Kevin Hendrey, and Sang-il Oum.
